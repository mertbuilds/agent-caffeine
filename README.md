# Agent Caffeine

> The brand is `Agent Caffeine`, with both words capitalised. Every
> user-visible string uses it: the app, the dmg and the site. The app
> bundle stays `agent caffeine.app`, because the hooks of installed users
> point to that path. `caffeinagent` is the name of the repo, the binary, the bundle id
> and the domain (caffeinagent.com), and the Xcode target and source
> folder are `Caffeinagent`. Users never see those names as the brand.

macOS menu bar app that keeps your Mac awake while AI coding agents work. By default it releases sleep prevention when agents are idle or waiting for your input, so the Mac can nap when it's safe to.

## What it does

- Finds the agents that run on the Mac from the process table and a built-in catalog (Claude Code, Codex, opencode, Antigravity and about 45 more). Settings has one rule per agent (automatic, always counts as working, ignore) and a list for process names of tools that are not in the catalog.
- Reads what each agent says about itself where it can: Claude Code's session state files and Codex's session logs give working, waiting for you, or idle. Other agents are judged by signs of work: a running command, a background task, a power assertion of their own, writes to their session files and, as weak evidence, processor use.
- Takes the state from an agent's hooks when you switch them on (see Hooks below). A fresh hook state comes before everything else.
- Three modes, picked in the menu or in Settings:
  - Always: keeps the Mac awake until you switch it off, agents or not.
  - While an agent is open: keeps the Mac awake while any agent is detected, working or idle.
  - While an agent is working (default): keeps the Mac awake while an agent works, for a short time after it finishes (2 minutes by default), and for a while when it waits for you (5 minutes by default).
- While keeping the Mac awake it holds `PreventUserIdleSystemSleep` and `PreventSystemSleep` assertions, and releases them as soon as the mode no longer calls for them. The assertions carry a 2 minute timeout that every decision renews, so a hung app cannot keep the Mac awake.
- After macOS says the Mac goes to sleep, Agent Caffeine starts no hold and takes no sleep flag until the Mac woke in full (macOS says so, or the displays come on) and a sample from after that wake was taken. A sleeping Mac wakes by itself with the display off many times, and what the app knew before the sleep (a pause that ended in the night, a state that still says "working") starts nothing there. A hold that runs is not broken by the word of sleep alone: it ends when a hole between two samples shows that the Mac did sleep. The rule is `Caffeinagent/Detection/WakeGate.swift`.
- Lets the Mac sleep when the battery is low (20% by default) or the Mac gets too hot, and says so in a notification. The battery floor stops every hold on battery, with the lid open or closed (`Caffeinagent/Power/SafetyMonitor.swift`). It also stops a hold on a charger that is too weak to hold the level (a phone charger, a power bank): macOS then still reports wall power, so the floor counts when the level fell by 2 points or more while on power.
- "Let your Mac sleep when the battery is low" (Settings > Battery and lid, on by default) switches the battery floor off, after one question ("Let the battery run empty?"). With it off, Agent Caffeine keeps the Mac awake until the battery is empty: the Mac then turns off at once, and work that is not saved can be lost. A notification says so once at 10% and once at 5% while Agent Caffeine keeps the Mac awake on battery. The stop for heat has no switch.
- Battery line: while the Mac runs on battery and Agent Caffeine could keep it awake, the menu shows one small line below the headline: a battery mark filled to the charge, the charge, and what follows from it ("58% · sleeps below 20%"). The mark's level and the number are amber within 10 points of the floor, and the mark and the number are red with "starts again at 25%" while the floor stopped Agent Caffeine; the menu then has no second line that says the same. With the battery floor off the line says "no battery limit", and it is amber at 10% and below and red at 5% and below. It is hidden on wall power, on a Mac without a battery, and while Agent Caffeine is off or paused.
- Durations read as a person says them: "22 minutes", "1 hour 22 minutes" up to 10 hours, then "13 hours", then "3 days" (`Caffeinagent/Detection/HumaneDuration.swift`).
- Surfaces state in the menu bar with a small version of the robot of the app icon, in the same pose as the robot in the menu: a solid head with smiling eyes when keeping the Mac awake, round eyes while an agent is waiting for you, a grey outline with closed eyes when the Mac can sleep or Agent Caffeine is paused or off, and red crosses for eyes after a safety stop. The menu names what keeps the Mac awake and lists each agent with its state and the reason.
- "Show Agent Caffeine in the menu bar" (Settings > General, on by default) hides the icon. The app keeps your Mac awake the same way without it. To open Settings again, open Agent Caffeine from Applications or Spotlight. While the icon is hidden, a notification says when the helper was turned off, the helper cannot be reached or the license no longer holds.
- Pause: "Pause for 30 minutes", "Pause for 1 hour" or "Pause until tomorrow" (06:00, or 8 hours when the morning is less than 4 hours away), from the timer button next to the switch. While paused Agent Caffeine holds nothing and clears the lid closed setting, as if it were off, and starts again by itself. The end is a time on the clock ("Paused until 15:30") and is saved, so it lasts over a restart of the app, and the time the Mac sleeps counts: a Mac that slept past the end wakes with the pause over. Switching Agent Caffeine off ends a pause, and "Resume" ends it at once. The rule is `Caffeinagent/Detection/Pause.swift`.
- Stay reachable: "Stay reachable for 1 hour", "for 2 hours" or "for 4 hours", from the same timer button. It is the mirror of a pause: until the time on the clock ("Reachable until 18:30") the Mac stays awake also when no agent works, and with the lid closed on battery when the helper is set up, so a message from your phone (Claude Code Remote Control, Codex remote) reaches the agent in seconds. An agent that works keeps its own line in the menu. It costs about 1% of battery per hour on the Mac it was measured on (`docs/research/07-display-and-remote-wake.md`), and the menu says so on battery. "Stop" ends it, a pause ends it, switching Agent Caffeine off ends it, and a safety stop ends it for good: it does not start again when the power returns. With the battery floor off only heat is a safety stop, so the battery does not end it. Starting it ends a pause. The end is saved like the end of a pause. The rule is `Caffeinagent/Detection/Reachable.swift`.
- Agent notifications (Settings > Notifications), each with its own switch: "An agent is waiting for you" (on by default) and "An agent finished" (off by default), both with the system sound. A session finished when its turn ends or its process is gone, also when a command it started runs on, and not when it works again within 2 seconds. It waits when it asks a question or needs a permission and still does so after 3 seconds. Each change gives one notification with an identifier of its own, and the older one about the same session leaves Notification Center. "Only notify when the agent's window is not in front" (on by default) skips the notification while you use the app the agent runs in. Three or more agents that change within 2 seconds give one notification. Nothing is told for what was already there at launch, or while Agent Caffeine is off or paused. A click on a notification about one agent brings the app that agent runs in to the front, looked up at the click. A click on one about several agents does what macOS does by itself. macOS asks for permission the first time one is due or when you switch one on. Settings > Notifications lists the last 5 notifications and what became of them, and every decision is in the log with the pid, the state and the time, and no name: `log show --last 1h --info --predicate 'subsystem == "com.caffeinagent.app" AND category == "Notices"'`. `caffeinagent --lab-notices <recording>` replays a lab recording and lists what would be told. The notifications about a safety stop, and the ones at 10% and at 5% with the battery floor off, have no switch. The name of a session can come from what you typed: it is shown in the notification on your Mac and is never logged or recorded. The rules are `Caffeinagent/Detection/AgentNotices.swift`.
- "Open Agent Caffeine at login" (Settings > General) registers the app with `SMAppService.mainApp`, only from `/Applications`. The last page of the setup window asks with a checked box, and nothing is registered when the setup is skipped. The state mapping is `Caffeinagent/LoginItem.swift`.
- A stop has its own headline, "Agent Caffeine has stopped", with the reason below it (the battery, heat, the trial, the license), so a row that says "Working" below it is no contradiction.
- After a sleep under an agent the menu says why, once, until it is dismissed or the Mac sleeps again: "Your Mac slept at 02:14: the battery was at 20%". The app takes what it knows when macOS says the Mac goes to sleep, with the lid read at that moment, and names only reasons that are certain: a stop, a pause, a license that does not hold, a wait that ran out, and a lid closed on battery without the helper. A Mac that the user put to sleep by hand gets no line. The rule is `Caffeinagent/Detection/SleepReason.swift`.
- A click on an agent in the menu brings the app it runs in to the front and closes the menu: the agent's own app, or the terminal or editor its command line runs in. That is the app, not the window or the tab. An app whose windows are all in the Dock shows one again, as after a click on its icon in the Dock. A line of several sessions opens its group, and each session below it goes to its own app. An agent with no app to go to takes no click: over SSH, under screen or zellij, or under a tmux or herdr with no client, or with clients in more than one app. The rules are `Caffeinagent/Detection/AgentHost.swift`.
- A right click on an agent in the menu goes to its app ("Go to Cursor"), sets its rule ("This agent is working", "Ignore this agent") or opens its settings.
- Keyboard shortcuts that work in every app (Settings > Shortcuts): "Turn Agent Caffeine on or off", which is the switch and ends a pause, and "Pause for 1 hour". The defaults are ⌃⌥⌘C and ⌃⌥⌘P; you can record others, clear them, or restore the default. They are registered with Carbon's `RegisterEventHotKey`, which needs no Accessibility permission. A shortcut needs Command, Control or Option, and one that macOS uses is refused (`Caffeinagent/Shortcut/`).

- On power, stays awake while a tool's phone access is on, also when no agent works (see Phone access below).

The detection code is in `Caffeinagent/Detection/`, and the research behind it in `docs/research/`. `caffeinagent --lab-record <label>` records what the engine sees without starting the app (see `Caffeinagent/LabCommand.swift`). The app binary starts the app only without arguments: the lab commands, `--display-probe` and `--smoke-test` (see Before a release) are the only arguments it takes, and it refuses any other.

## Hooks

Some agents can run a command when their state changes. With hooks on, an agent tells Agent Caffeine when it works, waits for you or is done, so nothing has to be inferred. Hooks are off until you switch them on, per agent, in Settings > Agents > Better detection or in the setup window.

- Supported: Claude Code and Codex (adds "waiting for your permission"), Droid, opencode and Pi (adds working, waiting and done). Pi has no permission step of its own, so it waits only when one of its extensions asks you something. `Caffeinagent/Detection/Hooks/HookCatalog.swift` holds the list, what each entry was confirmed by, and the agents that are left out and why.
- Switching hooks on adds a few lines to the agent's own config file (`~/.claude/settings.json`, `~/.codex/hooks.json`, `~/.factory/settings.json`), or writes one file of its own for opencode (`~/.config/opencode/plugins/agent-caffeine.js`) and for Pi (`~/.pi/agent/extensions/agent-caffeine.js`). The rest of the file stays as it is, a backup is kept next to it, and switching hooks off removes exactly those lines again.
- The lines run `CaffeinagentHook`, a small command inside the app in `/Applications`, never the app itself. It reads the event, writes one small state file per agent process to `~/Library/Application Support/com.caffeinagent.app/hooks/`, prints nothing and always exits 0. It keeps the state and the name of the event, and nothing you or the agent typed. The app does not have to run, and the lines do nothing when the app is gone.
- Hooks need the app in `/Applications`, because the lines point there.

## Command line and MCP

A script, a long job or an agent that Agent Caffeine does not know can ask for the Mac to stay awake, and let it go again. The tool is `agent-caffeine`, inside the app at `Contents/MacOS/agent-caffeine`. Settings > Agents > Command line has "Set up for agents" (see below), and for a setup by hand it shows the path of the tool, the command that links it into `~/.local/bin`, and the entry for an agent's MCP servers.

- `agent-caffeine hold --for 30m [--reason "text"]` keeps the Mac awake for a time: `90s`, `30m`, `2h`. One hour with no `--for`, 12 hours at most.
- `agent-caffeine hold [--reason "text"] -- <command> [args...]` runs the command, keeps the Mac awake while it runs, and ends with the exit code of the command. A signal sent to the tool is passed on to the command. The hold ends after 24 hours at the latest, and the command runs on.
- `agent-caffeine release [<id> | --all]` ends a hold. `agent-caffeine status [--json]` lists the holds and says whether the Mac can sleep.
- `agent-caffeine skill` prints the skill of the app (`skills/agent-caffeine/SKILL.md` of this repo), so any agent can read it. The tool installs nothing.
- `agent-caffeine mcp` is an MCP server on stdin and stdout with three tools: `hold_awake` (`minutes` from 1 to 720, `reason`), `release` (`id`, or the holds of the session) and `status`. A hold ends when its session ends: when the agent closes the server's input or its output, or ends the tool with a signal, the holds that session made are released.
- A hold is one small file in `~/Library/Application Support/com.caffeinagent.app/holds/`, which the app reads with every sample and watches. No daemon and no socket. A timed hold is dead at its time. A command hold is dead when the tool that holds it is gone, checked by pid and start time, so a pid that is used again keeps nothing awake. A dead hold is removed. While the tool is stopped (`kill -STOP`, Ctrl-Z) its hold keeps nothing awake, and it counts again when the tool is continued.
- A hold is a reason to stay awake like an agent at work, and goes through the same decision. The battery limit, heat, a pause, the switch and a trial that ended still let the Mac sleep. In the menu each hold has a line with "Stop".
- `hold --for` is refused while the app does not run. `hold -- <command>` runs the command all the same and says so on stderr.
- `AGENT_CAFFEINE_HOME` names another folder in place of the app's own. It is for tests and development.

### Set up for agents

An agent uses the tool when a skill tells it to. "Set up for agents" in Settings > Agents > Command line, and in the setup window, does that with one click:

- It writes the skill as `<skills folder>/agent-caffeine/SKILL.md` for each agent whose folder is on the Mac: Claude Code (`~/.claude/skills`), Codex and Pi (`~/.agents/skills`), opencode, Cursor, Gemini CLI, Droid, Grok Build, Amp, goose and OpenClaw. An agent that reads a folder the skill is already in gets no second copy, so with Claude Code and Codex on the Mac the skill is in two folders and every agent of the list reads one of them. `Caffeinagent/Detection/Skills/SkillCatalog.swift` holds the folders, what each was confirmed by and the agents that are left out, and `docs/research/13-agent-skills.md` the research.
- It links the tool as `~/.local/bin/agent-caffeine`, from the copy in `/Applications` only: a copy that runs from another folder writes the skill and says to move the app there. A link to a copy of the app that is gone reads as not linked, and "Link" makes it again. When `~/.local/bin` does not seem to be on the PATH of your login shell, Settings shows the line to add. The app tells that from the startup files of the shell, `/etc/paths` and `/etc/paths.d`, which it only reads: it runs no shell. The skill also names the full path of the tool, so it works without the link.
- It writes its own folder `agent-caffeine` and that one link, and nothing else. A folder, a file or a link of that name that Agent Caffeine did not make is left as it is, and Settings says so. That holds for a link to something that is gone too: only a link into a copy of the app that is gone is made again. A skills folder that is a link to a place outside the agent's folder is not written to. The file it writes has the line `<!-- agent-caffeine-skill v=1 -->` after the frontmatter, which is how the app knows its own.
- Settings then lists each agent with its state, with "Install" next to an agent that came to the Mac later and "Update" next to a skill of an older version. At launch the app replaces a skill of its own from an older version, only while nobody edited it, and points the link at the copy in `/Applications` when the app made that link or the copy it points at is gone. A link you made by hand to another copy stays. It installs nothing new by itself.
- "Remove" takes out the files, the link and the folders the app made, each folder only when it is empty. What it made is recorded in `~/Library/Application Support/com.caffeinagent.app/skill-installs/record.json`.
- A change of the text of the skill needs a new `SkillCatalog.version` and its digest in `CaffeinagentTests/SkillTextTests.swift`.

## Setup window

The first launch opens a setup window once. It names the agents it found, offers the hooks of the agents that have them, offers "Set up for agents" when an agent that reads skills is on the Mac, and offers the lid closed helper on a Mac with a battery. Every step can be skipped, and the app works the same while the window is open. Everything in it is also in Settings.

## Phone access

Claude Code Remote Control lets you work with a session on this Mac from a phone or the web, and that only works while the Mac is awake. With "Stay reachable while plugged in" (Settings > General, on by default), the Mac stays awake on power while Remote Control is on, also when no agent works. The menu then says "Reachable while plugged in". On battery this does not hold the Mac awake: "Stay reachable" in the menu is the timed version that you start yourself and that also works on battery.

Remote Control counts as on for a `claude remote-control` server, for a session started with `--remote-control` or `--rc`, and for every interactive session when `remoteControlAtStartup` is true in `~/.claude/settings.json`. A session that turns it on later with `/remote-control` is not seen. No other tool has a detector yet (`Caffeinagent/Detection/RemoteAccessDetector.swift`).

## Lid closed on battery

`kIOPMAssertionTypePreventSystemSleep` keeps the Mac awake with the **lid closed only on AC power**. On battery, macOS sleeps on lid close regardless, and an assertion alone cannot override that. Only the system-wide `SleepDisabled` setting does, and it needs root.

The app sets it through a small root helper (`CaffeinagentHelper/`), registered with `SMAppService` from Settings > Battery and lid and approved once in System Settings > General > Login Items. The helper is offered only when the app runs from `/Applications`. It takes two booleans over XPC from the signed app and nothing else. The app sets the flag together with the power assertions, also on AC, so unplugging with the lid closed does not race it, and sends a heartbeat. The helper clears the flag when the heartbeats stop, at its own start, and when launchd stops it, and never clears a flag it did not set. After an update of the app the helper that still runs is the old binary: the app asks it to leave, and launchd starts the new one on the next call with no change of the registration. A helper that does not know that call is removed and registered again. `SleepDisabled` also blocks the kernel's low-battery and thermal sleep, which is why the app watches both itself.

### The display under a closed lid

"Turn the display off when the lid is closed" (Settings > Battery and lid, on by default) keeps the built-in display dark while Agent Caffeine keeps the Mac awake with the lid closed. The rules are `Caffeinagent/Power/DisplayRules.swift`:

- No external display: the displays are put to sleep at once (`IORequestIdle` on `IODisplayWrangler`, the request `pmset displaysleepnow` makes, and that command when the request is refused), and again when something wakes them under the lid, at most once per 5 seconds. When sleep does not hold, the brightness of the built-in display also goes to zero. Keys or a pointer that move under the closed lid mean someone uses the Mac from afar: for a minute after them the display is only dimmed, never put to sleep.
- With an external display: display sleep would take the display you work on, so only the brightness of the built-in display goes to zero.
- The brightness is set through the private `DisplayServices` framework, then IOKit's `IODisplaySetFloatParameter`, then `CoreDisplay`, whichever works first. Your value is saved before the display goes dark and put back when the lid opens, when the setting goes off, when a stop ends the hold, when macOS says the Mac goes to sleep, when Agent Caffeine quits, and at the next launch after a crash. Putting it back never makes the display darker than it is.

"Play a sound when you close the lid and your Mac stays awake" (Settings > Battery and lid, on by default) plays a short system sound once when the lid closes while Agent Caffeine keeps the Mac awake: on wall power the hold is enough, on battery the helper must have confirmed the sleep flag, for which the sound waits up to 1.5 seconds. No sound means the Mac goes to sleep. The rules are `Caffeinagent/Power/LidChime.swift`.

The lid and the list of displays are watched (`kIOPMMessageClamshellStateChange` of the power root domain and the display reconfiguration callback), and the rules run in that callback: the display goes off and comes back at once, not at the next sample of the engine.

"Dim the display as the lid closes" (under it, on by default, shown only on a Mac with a lid angle sensor) makes the brightness follow the lid angle. The rules are `Caffeinagent/Power/LidDimming.swift`:

- At 45 degrees and above the brightness is yours and is not touched. From 45 degrees down to 3 degrees it is your brightness times a smoothstep curve of the angle (half at 24 degrees). At 3 degrees and below, or when the lid switch says closed, it is zero, and without an external display the displays also go to sleep. Opening is the same curve the other way: the displays wake and the brightness rises at the first reading above 3 degrees.
- The sensor gives whole degrees, and one degree is up to 3.6% of the brightness. So the curve is fed from an angle without steps (`LidAngleEstimator.swift`): it walks to each new value of the sensor at the speed the lid was measured to have, never passes it, and leaves out one degree of noise. The brightness is written up to 120 times a second, in steps of 0.1% or more.
- Your brightness is read when the lid comes below 45 degrees, and the fade stays on that value: what auto-brightness does under a lid that covers the light sensor is not taken for yours. A brightness key that you press while the lid rests between 20 and 45 degrees gives the new value (`BrightnessKeyDetector.swift`).
- A lid that rests between 20 and 45 degrees for 1.5 seconds is in use: your brightness comes back, and the fade starts again when the lid moves down.
- It works while Agent Caffeine keeps the Mac awake. When it does not, the Mac sleeps at lid close and macOS turns the display off. A fade that started is finished the same way when the hold ends in the middle.
- The sensor is a HID device of the Mac (vendor 0x05AC, product 0x8104, usage page 0x0020, usage 0x008A) that needs no permission. It is asked every 0.5 seconds while Agent Caffeine keeps the Mac awake, 120 times a second while the lid moves below 70 degrees, and not at all otherwise. The brightness is written in the same step that reads the angle. When the sensor gives no answer for 0.5 seconds, your brightness comes back.
- With the setting off, or on a Mac without the sensor, the display goes off when the lid switch says closed.

`caffeinagent --display-probe <seconds>` prints what the lid, the lid angle sensor, each display, the brightness readers and the keyboard backlight say: once per second, and 20 times a second while the lid is below 70 degrees. The `lidEstimate` column is the angle without the steps of the sensor, and `wouldSet` is the brightness the fade would set. It only reads, and starts no app.

To see what a fade did, switch the trace on: `defaults write com.caffeinagent.app Display.debugTrace -bool YES`. The running app reads the switch at the start of each fade, so it needs no restart. Each close and each open of the lid then leaves `display-trace-<time>.tsv` in `~/Library/Application Support/com.caffeinagent.app/`, written when the lid is steady for 2 seconds: one line per step with the seconds since the start, the angle of the sensor, the angle without steps, the brightness the fade wants, the brightness it set, the brightness read back after it, and a 1 in `fight` when that is more than 1% from what the fade set last (macOS set its own). A file has 2000 lines at most and the newest 5 files are kept. It holds angles and brightness only. `defaults delete com.caffeinagent.app Display.debugTrace` switches it off. The probe writes the same file for its dry run, with no value in `written`.

Builds before the helper used a sudoers entry for `pmset -b disablesleep`. The app clears a flag such a build left on at first launch, and Settings offers to remove the entry.

## Build

Requires Xcode 16+, macOS 13+ deployment target.

```bash
brew install xcodegen          # one-time
xcodegen generate              # regenerate Xcode project from project.yml
xcodebuild -project Caffeinagent.xcodeproj -scheme Caffeinagent -configuration Debug -derivedDataPath build
open build/Build/Products/Debug/caffeinagent.app
```

Run the unit tests with `xcodebuild test -project Caffeinagent.xcodeproj -scheme Caffeinagent -destination 'platform=macOS'`.

## Releases and updates

The app updates itself with [Sparkle](https://sparkle-project.org). It reads the feed at `https://caffeinagent.com/mac/appcast.xml` once a day, and from "Check for updates…" in Settings. Debug builds never check.

- `scripts/release.sh` is the only way to publish a release. It builds, signs, notarizes, writes the dmg, signs it for Sparkle, adds it to the appcast, uploads the dmg, `appcast.xml` and `latest.json` to `mac/` in the R2 bucket the site serves (named in `~/.config/agent-caffeine/release.env`, public to read at `https://files.caffeinagent.com`), and publishes the dmg to Polar. `--dry-run` does everything local and uploads, notarizes and publishes nothing. A release stops at the start when the working tree has changes that are not committed (`--dry-run --allow-dirty` builds them all the same, and `scripts/build-on-request.sh` uses that), runs the unit tests before it builds, and asks Gatekeeper (`spctl`) about the notarized app and the dmg before the first upload. The header of the script lists the one-time setup.
- Updates are signed with the EdDSA key in the login keychain under the account `agent-caffeine`. Its public half is `SUPublicEDKey` in `project.yml`. Every Sparkle tool call passes `--account agent-caffeine`.
- Run the script from a local Terminal: the keychain refuses SSH and scripted sessions.
- Third-party licenses are in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` and `Resources/Licenses/`, both bundled into the app.

## Before a release

Run `scripts/smoke-test.sh`. The unit tests never start the app, so they pass on a build that hangs at launch. The smoke test builds the app and starts it for 14 seconds as `caffeinagent --smoke-test 14`: the real scenes, the real aggregator and the real engine, with no power assertion, helper, Keychain, Polar, Sparkle or notification behind them, and with settings of its own, so it is safe next to the copy you use. It hides and shows the icon and opens Settings once in its first seconds, takes no click, and fails when the main thread stalls, the app uses 15% of a core or more in the median steady second (the seconds after the first 2, without the ones of those two steps) or 40% or more over the steady seconds, the scenes are worked out more than 50 times per second, the menu bar has no icon or the engine takes no sample. For its last 6 seconds it shows the views of the menu in a window that is on no screen, with a robot that floats and agents at work, and fails when the app uses 12% of a core or more in the median of those seconds: the motion of the menu runs in Core Animation and costs the app next to nothing, and a motion that the app works out for each frame takes 20% or more. It prints what it measured as one line of JSON, then PASS or FAIL. `scripts/smoke-test.sh --app <path>` tests a bundle that is built already, and `scripts/release.sh` runs it on the signed app and stops when it fails, also with `--dry-run`.

## License keys

The app runs fully for 7 days from its first launch. The last page of the setup window says so, a notification says once when the last day begins and once when the trial has ended, with the icon shown or hidden, and these two have no switch. After that it keeps the Mac awake only with a license key, sold on Polar. A key runs on one Mac ($19) or on three Macs ($29): the plan sets the number of Polar activations, and Settings > License names it after the activation. The app activates, validates and deactivates keys through Polar's public customer portal endpoints, sandbox in Debug and production in Release (`Caffeinagent/License/LicenseConfig.swift`). The key, its activation id and the trial clock live in the login Keychain (service `com.caffeinagent.app.license`, `.debug` appended in Debug builds). The trial start, the latest time seen and the date of the last successful check are mirrored in `UserDefaults` in case the Keychain cannot be read; the key and the activation id are not. A licensed Mac stays licensed offline for 30 days after its last successful check.
